# Casio ClassPad 300

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia.

The **Casio ClassPad 300**, **ClassPad 330** and **fx-CP400** are stylus based touch-screen [graphing calculators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphing_calculator). It comes with a collection of applications that support self-study, like 3D Graph, Geometry, eActivity, Spreadsheet, etc. A large 160x240 pixel LCD touch screen enables stylus-based operation. It resembles Casio's earlier [Pocket Viewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_Computer) line.

The ClassPad 300 allows input of expressions, and displays them as they appear in a textbook. Factorization of expressions, calculation of limit values of functions, and other operations can be performed while viewing the results on a large LCD screen. It also comes with graphing tools for 3D graphing and drawing of geometric figures.

The user interface features a pull-down menu format. Solutions, expressions, and other items can be selected with the tap of the stylus. Drag and drop, copy and paste, and other pen-based operations, are also supported. An eActivity application allows the creation of so-called eActivities, which can include figures, expressions, and explanations.

In the United States the ClassPad series is banned from standardized tests including the [SAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT), the [ACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_(test)), and the AP Calculus test, due to its virtual QWERTY keyboard and stylus usage.

## History

During 1996, CASIO worked on the CAS (Computer Algebra System) and studying Geometry. The CAS was first used in the Casio CFX-9970G then the Casio Algebra FX 2.0, and later formed the core math system for the ClassPad.

In 1999, the idea of the eActivity emerged. It was intended to allow all applications to interact from within one application, and display information in a textbook style.

- In 2002, CASIO completed a prototype for the ClassPad. The emulator was later included in the ClassPad Manager software.
- In 2003 and 2005, CASIO released the ClassPad 300 with 4.5 MB of flash memory, and the ClassPad 300 Plus with 5.4 MB.
- In 2006 CASIO released OS 3.0: Laplace and Fourier transformation, differential equation graphs, financial functions and parameterized 3D graphs.
- In 2012, CASIO released the ClassPad 330 Plus featuring a faster CPU (SuperH 4) and USB mass storage.

### ClassPad OS 3.0

Later in 2006 and 2007 CASIO released OS 3.01 and 3.02, concentrating solely on bug fixing. The 2007 ClassPad was called the 330 series, distinguished from the 300 plus only by having OS 3.02 pre-installed. OS 3.03 featured new probability distribution functions, an extended numeric solver and several user interface improvements.

## fx-CP400

The fx-CP400, released in 2013, has a colour screen also capable of switching between portrait and landscape views. The resolution was also improved, to 320 x 528 pixels.

| CPU | SuperH 4 (SH7305) |
| RAM | 2 MB (512 KB user) |

The CPU is a SuperH 4 of the SH7305 model. It contains a 2MB RAM chip, but only 512KB are available to the user. It includes 24 MB of flash storage, and 5.5 MB for eActivity.

## fx-CG500

The Casio fx-CG500 was introduced in 2017, and is almost identical to the fx-CP400. The most significant difference is that its virtual keyboard is stuck in alphabetical (ABC) layout. Due to this, it is now included in the list of authorised models in American SAT and AP exams.

## Programming

The calculators can be programmed in two ways. The ClassPad comes with Casio BASIC, a built-in BASIC-like interpreted language. The other method is to create an add-in. Add-ins are binary programs, executing directly on the calculator's CPU.

After the release of the SDK, a Lua interpreter plugin (CPlua) was created. This add-in allows users to develop programs and games in Lua.
